# Cold outreach email

A cold email earns a reply by being relevant and easy to answer. Relevance comes from a real, recent reason to write; ease comes from a short email with one low-friction question.

## Setup (fill in once)
- SENDER_NAME: {{your first name}}
- COMPANY_NAME: {{your company}}
- WHAT_WE_DO: {{one plain sentence, no buzzwords}}
- PROOF_POINT: {{one real, verifiable result or client you may cite, or "none"}}
- SIGNATURE: does the email client add it automatically? {{yes/no}}
- LANGUAGE: {{language for external emails}}

If any of these are still placeholders, ask for them once before drafting.

## Inputs
Ask only for what is missing:
- Prospect first name, role, company, country
- A real, recent, checkable trigger (news, hire, launch, job post, event, public statement)
- The one problem of theirs we can plausibly help with

## Steps
1. Search the web for the company and contact. Find one real, recent trigger and note its source.
2. If none exists, say so and write a relevance-based email built on their role or industry problem. A made-up trigger gets spotted quickly and burns the sender's credibility.
3. Pick one problem and one angle. Extra angles dilute the question.
4. Draft one email in LANGUAGE, adjusting tone to the prospect's country.
5. Check against the rules, then deliver with flags: the trigger and its source, assumptions, and anything the sender must verify.

## Format
- Subject: 2–5 words, specific, no hype. Avoid "Quick question" and "Partnership", which read as templates.
- Greeting: "Hi [First name],"
- Line 1: the real trigger or observation about them, without flattery.
- Line 2: the problem it usually creates, plus one concrete sentence on how we help (WHAT_WE_DO).
- Line 3 (optional): PROOF_POINT, only if real.
- Ask: one interest question ("Is this on your radar?"). Leave out the calendar link in a first touch, because it asks for time before any interest exists.
- Stay under about 90 words, in short sentences.

## Rules, and why
- **One variant only.**
- **Only verifiable facts.** Never invent numbers, clients, mutual connections or results; they are easy to check and fatal when wrong.
- **No ROI claims, hype words or "I hope this finds you well".** They mark the email as a template.
- **No pricing, attachments or budget questions.** Those come after interest is confirmed.
- **Don't pretend a relationship exists** or fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subjects. Deceptive subjects damage trust and may break the law in some countries.
- **Unsolicited-email rules differ by country** (for example GDPR / ePrivacy in the EU, prior-consent requirements in some states, CAN-SPAM in the US). This skill does not verify legality: the sender must check the rules for the recipient's country and include an easy opt-out.
- **If the prospect looks like a private individual rather than a business contact, stop and ask the user.**
